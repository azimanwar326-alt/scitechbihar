Periodic Comets Non-Periodic Comets

6. Define the Following Terms


Question 1. What is a star?

Answer: A star is a huge ball of hot gases that produces its own heat and light. The Sun is the nearest star to Earth.


Question 2. What is a constellation?

Answer: A constellation is a group of stars that appears to form a particular pattern in the night sky. Examples are Ursa Major and Orion.


Question 3. What is a comet?

Answer: A comet is a small celestial body made mainly of ice, dust and rocky material. It revolves around the Sun and may develop a bright tail when it comes near the Sun.


Question 4. What is an asteroid?

Answer: An asteroid is a small rocky object that revolves around the Sun. Most asteroids are found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.


Question 5. What is light pollution?

Answer: Light pollution is the excessive or unnecessary use of artificial light at night. It makes it difficult to see stars and other objects in the night sky.


7. Answer the Following Questions Briefly


Question 1. What is a star? Why can't we see some stars with our naked eyes?

Answer: A star is a huge ball of hot gases that produces its own heat and light.

We cannot see some stars with our naked eyes because:

  1. They are very far away from Earth.
  2. They appear very faint.
  3. Their light may be blocked by clouds or affected by light pollution.

Question 2. What is the solar system?

Answer: The solar system consists of the Sun and all the objects that revolve around it due to its gravitational force.

It includes:

  1. Eight planets.
  2. Their natural satellites.
  3. Dwarf planets.
  4. Asteroids.
  5. Comets.
  6. Meteoroids.

Question 3. Summarise the main features of the Sun.

Answer: The main features of the Sun are:

  1. The Sun is a star.
  2. It is made mainly of hot gases.
  3. It produces its own heat and light.
  4. It is the nearest star to Earth.
  5. It is the centre of our solar system.
  6. Its gravity keeps the planets moving around it.
  7. It provides energy needed for life on Earth.

Question 4. How can you distinguish the Polar Star from the other stars in the sky?

Answer: The Polar Star, also called Polaris, appears almost fixed in the northern sky. It helps us find the north direction.

We can locate it with the help of the Ursa Major constellation. The two stars at the outer edge of the bowl of Ursa Major point towards the Polar Star.

Question 5. What is night pollution?

Answer: Night pollution refers to excessive artificial light at night. It is commonly called light pollution. It makes the night sky bright and difficult to see stars clearly. It can also disturb the natural activities of animals and birds.

8. Answer the Following Questions in Detail

Question 1. Why does a meteoroid look like a shooting star?

Answer: A meteoroid is a small rocky or metallic object that moves through space.

When a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere at a very high speed, it experiences great friction with the air. This makes it very hot and causes it to glow.

It produces a bright streak of light in the sky, which looks like a shooting star.

Important Points:

  1. Meteoroids move through space.
  2. They enter Earth's atmosphere at high speeds.
  3. They become very hot due to interaction with the air.
  4. They glow and produce a bright streak of light.
  5. This bright streak is called a meteor or shooting star.

Conclusion: A shooting star is not a real star. It is the bright light produced when a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere.

Question 2. Write about the characteristics of the Moon in detail.

Answer: The Moon is the natural satellite of Earth. It is the nearest celestial body to our planet.

The main characteristics of the Moon are:

  1. Natural Satellite: The Moon revolves around Earth.
  2. No Own Light: The Moon does not produce its own light. It shines by reflecting sunlight.
  3. Surface: Its surface has mountains, plains, craters and valleys.
  4. No Air: The Moon has almost no atmosphere, so humans cannot breathe there without special equipment.
  5. Water: Water ice has been found in some permanently shadowed regions near the Moon's poles.
  6. Gravity: The Moon's gravity is weaker than Earth's gravity.
  7. Phases: The Moon appears to change its shape during the month. These changes are called the phases of the Moon.
  8. Revolution: The Moon takes about 27.3 days to complete one revolution around Earth.

Conclusion: The Moon is an important natural satellite of Earth. It influences ocean tides and is the brightest natural object in our night sky.

Question 3. Give a brief account of the eight planets in the solar system.

Answer: There are eight planets in our solar system. They revolve around the Sun in their own orbits.

1. Mercury

  • It is the nearest planet to the Sun.
  • It is the smallest planet in the solar system.
  • It has no natural satellite.

2. Venus

  • It is the second planet from the Sun.
  • It is the hottest planet in the solar system.
  • It is often called Earth's twin because of its similar size.

3. Earth

  • It is the third planet from the Sun.
  • It is the only known planet with life.
  • It has liquid water on its surface.
  • It is called the Blue Planet.

4. Mars

  • It is the fourth planet from the Sun.
  • It is called the Red Planet because of its reddish appearance.
  • It has two small natural satellites, Phobos and Deimos.

5. Jupiter

  • It is the fifth planet from the Sun.
  • It is the largest planet in the solar system.
  • It has a large storm called the Great Red Spot.

6. Saturn

  • It is the sixth planet from the Sun.
  • It is famous for its beautiful rings.
  • It is a gas giant.

7. Uranus

  • It is the seventh planet from the Sun.
  • It is an ice giant.
  • It rotates on its side compared with most other planets.

8. Neptune

  • It is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun.
  • It is an ice giant.
  • It has very strong winds.

Conclusion: The eight planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

Question 4. Explain how the Sun is the main source of energy for other planets and Earth.

Answer: The Sun is the main source of energy for Earth and an important source of energy for the other planets.

Its importance is explained below:

  1. Light and Heat: The Sun provides light and heat to Earth.
  2. Photosynthesis: Plants use sunlight to prepare their food.
  3. Water Cycle: Heat from the Sun causes water to evaporate and helps the water cycle.
  4. Weather and Climate: Solar energy affects the weather and climate of Earth.
  5. Life: Sunlight and warmth help make Earth suitable for life.
  6. Other Planets: The Sun provides light and heat to the other planets. Their surfaces and atmospheres receive different amounts of solar energy depending on their distance and other conditions.

Conclusion: Without the Sun's energy, life as we know it on Earth would not be possible.

Question 5. What is the name of our galaxy? Explain.

Answer: Our galaxy is called the Milky Way Galaxy.

The main features of the Milky Way are:

  1. It is a huge collection of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity.
  2. It contains billions of stars.
  3. Our solar system is located in the Milky Way.
  4. The Sun is one of the billions of stars in this galaxy.
  5. The Milky Way appears as a milky band of light in a dark night sky.
  6. It is a spiral galaxy.

Conclusion: The Milky Way is our home galaxy. Our Sun, Earth and the entire solar system are part of it.

9. Additional Important Questions and Answers

Question 1. What is the difference between rotation and revolution?

Answer:

Rotation Revolution
Rotation is the spinning of an object on its own axis. Revolution is the movement of an object around another object.
Earth takes about 24 hours to complete one rotation. Earth takes about 365¼ days to revolve around the Sun.
Rotation causes day and night. Earth's revolution around the Sun, along with its tilted axis, causes seasons.

Question 2. Why is Earth called the Blue Planet?

Answer: Earth is called the Blue Planet because a large part of its surface is covered with water. It appears blue when seen from space.

Question 3. What is an orbit?

Answer: An orbit is the fixed path along which a planet or satellite moves around another celestial body.

Question 4. What is the difference between a meteor and a meteorite?

Answer:

  • Meteor: A meteoroid that produces a bright streak of light when it enters Earth's atmosphere.
  • Meteorite: A part of a meteoroid that survives its journey through the atmosphere and reaches Earth's surface.

Question 5. What is the asteroid belt?

Answer: The asteroid belt is a region between Mars and Jupiter where many asteroids revolve around the Sun.

Question 6. Why do planets shine in the sky?

Answer: Planets shine because they reflect the light of the Sun. They do not produce their own light.

Question 7. What is the difference between a comet and an asteroid?

Answer:

Comet Asteroid
It is mainly made of ice, dust and rocky material. It is mainly made of rock and metal.
It may develop a bright tail near the Sun. It usually does not develop a tail.
Example: Halley's comet. Example: Vesta.

Question 8. What is Halley's comet?

Answer: Halley's comet is a periodic comet that comes near the Sun approximately every 76 years. It was last seen in 1986 and is expected to return in 2061.

Question 9. What is the Polar Star?

Answer: The Polar Star, or Polaris, is a star that appears almost fixed in the northern sky. It helps us identify the north direction.

Question 10. What are artificial satellites used for?

Answer: Artificial satellites are used for:

  1. Communication.
  2. Weather forecasting.
  3. Television broadcasting.
  4. Navigation.
  5. Studying Earth and space.

Question 11. What is the difference between a natural satellite and a planet?

Answer: A planet revolves around a star, while a natural satellite revolves around a planet. For example, Earth is a planet and the Moon is its natural satellite.

Question 12. Why should we reduce light pollution?

Answer: We should reduce light pollution because:

  1. It helps us see stars and planets clearly.
  2. It saves electricity.
  3. It protects the natural activities of birds and animals.
  4. It makes the night sky darker and more beautiful.

11. Chapter Summary

The universe contains countless stars, planets, moons, galaxies, comets and other celestial objects. The Sun is the nearest star to Earth and is the centre of our solar system. There are eight planets that revolve around the Sun.

Earth is the only planet currently known to support life. The Moon is its natural satellite. Other objects in space include asteroids, meteoroids and comets. Our solar system is a part of the Milky Way Galaxy.

We can observe many celestial objects in the night sky. However, excessive artificial lighting causes light pollution and makes it difficult to see them clearly.

Message of the Chapter

The universe is vast and full of wonders. We should observe the night sky, ask questions and continue learning about space.
