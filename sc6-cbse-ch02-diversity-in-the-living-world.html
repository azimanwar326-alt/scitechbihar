2. Important Questions and Answers from Every Topic

Topic 1: Diversity in Plants and Animals Around Us

Question 11. What is biodiversity?

Answer: Biodiversity is the variety of plants, animals and other living organisms found in the environment.

Question 12. Why is biodiversity important?

Answer: Biodiversity is important because every organism plays a role in nature. Plants provide food and shelter, while animals help in pollination, seed dispersal and maintaining the balance of nature.

Question 13. What is meant by diversity in the living world?

Answer: Diversity in the living world means the presence of different kinds of plants, animals and other living organisms.

Question 14. How do plants and animals depend on each other?

Answer: Plants provide food and shelter to animals. Animals help plants through pollination and seed dispersal. In this way, plants and animals depend on each other.

Topic 2: How to Group Plants and Animals

Question 15. Why do we classify plants and animals?

Answer: We classify plants and animals to organise them into groups based on their similarities and differences. This makes them easier to study and understand.

Question 16. What is classification?

Answer: Classification is the process of grouping organisms according to their common features and differences.

Question 17. Name some features used to classify plants.

Answer: Plants can be classified according to their height, stem, leaf venation, root system and seed type.

Question 18. Name some features used to classify animals.

Answer: Animals can be classified according to their body shape, size, structure, colour, habitat and movement.

Topic 3: Classification of Plants Based on Their Height

Question 19. What are herbs? Give examples.

Answer: Herbs are short plants with soft, green stems. They usually have few branches.

Examples: Coriander, mint, wheat and mustard.

Question 20. What are shrubs? Give examples.

Answer: Shrubs are medium-sized, bushy plants with woody stems and branches arising near the base.

Examples: Rose, hibiscus, lemon and lantana.

Question 21. What are trees? Give examples.

Answer: Trees are tall plants with a hard, thick, woody main stem called a trunk.

Examples: Neem, peepal, mango and jamun.

Question 22. Differentiate between herbs, shrubs and trees.

Answer: The differences are given below:

Feature Herbs Shrubs Trees
Height Usually short Medium-sized Usually tall
Stem Soft and green Woody and hard Thick, hard and woody
Branches Few or small Arise near the base Usually arise higher up
Examples Coriander, mint Rose, hibiscus Neem, mango

Topic 4: Classification of Plants Based on Their Stem

Question 23. What are creepers? Give examples.

Answer: Creepers are plants with weak stems that spread along the ground because they cannot stand upright.

Examples: Watermelon and pumpkin.

Question 24. What are climbers? Give examples.

Answer: Climbers are plants with weak stems that grow upwards with the support of other objects.

Examples: Money plant and pea plant.

Question 25. Differentiate between creepers and climbers.

Answer: Creepers spread along the ground, while climbers grow upwards with the support of other objects.

Question 26. What are tendrils?

Answer: Tendrils are thin, thread-like structures that help climbing plants attach themselves to a support.

Topic 5: Classification of Plants Based on Leaf Venation

Question 27. What is venation?

Answer: Venation is the arrangement of veins and smaller veinlets in a leaf.

Question 28. What is reticulate venation?

Answer: Reticulate venation is a pattern in which the veins form a network on the leaf.

Examples: Mango and peepal.

Question 29. What is parallel venation?

Answer: Parallel venation is a pattern in which the veins run parallel to one another.

Examples: Grass and banana.

Question 30. How can leaf venation help us identify a plant's root system?

Answer: In the general pattern described in this chapter, plants with reticulate venation usually have taproots, while plants with parallel venation usually have fibrous roots.

Topic 6: Classification of Plants Based on Their Roots

Question 31. What is a root?

Answer: A root is the part of a plant that usually grows underground and helps hold the plant firmly in the soil.

Question 32. What are the two main types of root systems?

Answer: The two main types are:

  1. Taproot system
  2. Fibrous root system

Question 33. What is a taproot? Give examples.

Answer: A taproot system has one main root that grows downwards, with smaller side roots branching from it.

Examples: Mustard, carrot and radish.

Question 34. What is a fibrous root system? Give examples.

Answer: A fibrous root system has many thin roots of similar size arising from the base of the stem.

Examples: Wheat, rice and grass.

Question 35. Differentiate between taproots and fibrous roots.

Answer:

Taproot System Fibrous Root System
One main root is present. Many thin roots are present.
Smaller roots branch from the main root. Roots are usually similar in size.
Example: Carrot Example: Wheat

Topic 7: Classification of Plants Based on Their Seeds

Question 36. What is a cotyledon?

Answer: A cotyledon is a seed leaf present inside a seed.

Question 37. What are monocot plants? Give examples.

Answer: Monocot plants have seeds with one cotyledon. They generally have parallel venation and fibrous roots.

Examples: Rice, wheat and maize.

Question 38. What are dicot plants? Give examples.

Answer: Dicot plants have seeds with two cotyledons. They generally have reticulate venation and taproots.

Examples: Pea, chickpea and mango.

Question 39. Differentiate between monocots and dicots.

Answer:

Feature Monocots Dicots
Cotyledons One Two
Leaf venation Usually parallel Usually reticulate
Root system Usually fibrous Usually taproot
Examples Rice, wheat Pea, chickpea

Question 40. How do animals move from one place to another?

Question 42. Why do animals have different ways of moving?

Answer: Animals have different body structures and live in different habitats. Their ways of moving help them find food, escape from predators and survive in their environments.

Topic 9: Plants and Animals in Different Surroundings

Question 44. What is a habitat?

Answer: A habitat is the natural place where an organism lives and gets food, water, shelter and suitable conditions for survival.

Question 45. What are the main types of habitats?

Answer: The three main types described in this chapter are:

  1. Terrestrial habitats
  2. Aquatic habitats
  3. Arboreal habitats

Question 46. What is a terrestrial habitat? Give examples.

Answer: A terrestrial habitat is a habitat found on land, such as forests, grasslands, deserts and mountains.

Examples of terrestrial animals: Lions, tigers, camels and elephants.

Question 47. What is an aquatic habitat? Give examples.

Answer: An aquatic habitat is a habitat in water, such as ponds, lakes, rivers, seas and oceans.

Examples of aquatic animals: Fish, dolphins and whales.

Question 48. What is an arboreal habitat? Give examples.

Answer: An arboreal habitat is a habitat in trees.

Examples: Monkeys and squirrels.

Question 50. Can an organism live in more than one type of habitat?

Answer: Yes. Some organisms can live in more than one type of habitat. For example, frogs live on land and in water, while crocodiles use both water and land.

Topic 10: Habitat and Adaptation

Question 51. What is adaptation?

Answer: Adaptation is a feature or change in the structure or behaviour of an organism that helps it survive in its habitat.

Question 52. Why do organisms need adaptations?

Answer: Organisms need adaptations to obtain food and water, protect themselves, tolerate environmental conditions and reproduce successfully.

Question 53. What are the basic needs provided by a habitat?

Answer: A habitat provides food, water, shelter, protection and a suitable place to live and reproduce.

Topic 11: Adaptations in Deserts

Question 54. What are the main features of a desert habitat?

Answer: Deserts receive very little rainfall. They often have hot, dry conditions and a shortage of water.

Question 55. How is a camel adapted to living in a desert?

Answer: A camel has long legs, broad padded feet, long eyelashes, closable nostrils and a hump that stores fat. It also conserves water by producing a small amount of urine.

Question 56. What is the function of a camel's hump?

Answer: The hump stores fat, which can be used as an energy reserve when food is scarce.

Question 57. How do a camel's feet help it walk on sand?

Answer: Its broad, padded feet spread its weight and help prevent it from sinking deeply into soft sand.

Question 58. How does a camel protect itself from sand?

Answer: Its long eyelashes help keep sand out of its eyes, and its nostrils can close to prevent sand from entering.

Topic 12: Adaptations in Cactus Plants

Question 59. What are xerophytes?

Answer: Xerophytes are plants adapted to survive in dry environments with little water.

Question 60. How is a cactus adapted to a desert habitat?

Answer: A cactus has the following adaptations:

  1. Its leaves are reduced to spines, which reduce water loss.
  2. Its green stem makes food through photosynthesis.
  3. Its thick stem stores water.
  4. A waxy outer layer reduces water loss.
  5. Its long roots can reach water deep in the soil.

Question 61. Why are cactus leaves changed into spines?

Answer: The leaves are changed into spines to reduce water loss through transpiration and to provide some protection from animals.

Question 62. How does the green stem of a cactus help it?

Answer: The green stem carries out photosynthesis and helps the plant make food.

Question 63. Why does a cactus have a thick, waxy stem?

Answer: The thick stem stores water, while the waxy layer reduces water loss.

Topic 13: Adaptations in Mountains

Question 64. What are the conditions in mountain habitats?

Answer: Mountain habitats are often cold and windy. The ground may be covered with snow, and plants may find it difficult to obtain water and nutrients.

Question 65. How is a mountain goat adapted to its habitat?

Answer: A mountain goat has strong, rough and flexible hooves that help it climb rocky slopes. Its thick fur keeps it warm, and its strong legs help it jump between rocks.

Question 66. How does the fur of a mountain goat help it?

Answer: Its thick fur protects it from cold weather and helps keep its body warm.

Question 67. How do mountain goats protect themselves from predators?

Answer: Their horns help protect them from predators, while their agility helps them escape across rocky slopes.

Question 68. How are plants adapted to mountain habitats?

Answer:

  1. Many mountain plants have short stems to withstand strong winds.
  2. Trees often have cone-shaped branches that allow snow to slide off.
  3. Needle-like leaves reduce water loss.
  4. Some trees remain green throughout the year.
  5. Thick bark helps protect trees from cold conditions.

Question 69. Why do some mountain trees have cone-shaped branches?

Answer: Their cone-shaped branches allow snow to slide off easily and reduce damage to the branches.

Topic 14: Adaptations in Freshwater Habitats

Question 70. What is a freshwater habitat?

Answer: A freshwater habitat is a habitat found in water with a low amount of dissolved salts, such as ponds, lakes and rivers.

Question 71. How is a fish adapted to living in water?

Answer: A fish has a streamlined body, fins and a tail for swimming, and gills for breathing underwater.

Question 72. What is a streamlined body?

Answer: A streamlined body is a shape that allows an organism to move through water or air with less resistance.

Question 73. What are amphibians? Give an example.

Answer: Amphibians are animals that can live both on land and in water. A frog is an example.

Question 74. How is a frog adapted to its habitat?

Answer: A frog has webbed feet for swimming, strong hind legs for jumping and moist skin that helps it exchange gases. Its lungs help it breathe on land.

Question 75. How are aquatic plants adapted to life in water?

Answer:

  1. Their roots are often reduced because water is readily available.
  2. Their stems may be long, hollow and flexible.
  3. Floating plants may have broad leaves.
  4. Submerged plants often have narrow leaves that bend with water movement.

Question 76. How do floating leaves of aquatic plants remain protected from water?

Answer: Many floating leaves have a waxy coating that helps repel water and protects them from decay.

Question 77. What are the adaptations of Hydrilla?

Answer: Hydrilla is a submerged aquatic plant. It has thin leaves and flexible stems that help it remain underwater and move with water currents.

Topic 15: Saltwater Habitats

Question 78. What is a saltwater habitat?

Answer: A saltwater habitat is a habitat containing water with a high concentration of dissolved salts, such as seas and oceans.

Question 79. Name some animals found in saltwater habitats.

Answer: Fish, dolphins, whales, molluscs and sea anemones are found in saltwater habitats.

Question 80. How do dolphins and whales breathe?

Answer: Dolphins and whales breathe air through blowholes on the upper parts of their heads. They must come to the water's surface to breathe.

Question 81. How are dolphins and whales adapted to aquatic life?

Answer: They have streamlined bodies for swimming, flippers for movement and blowholes for breathing air. They can remain underwater for some time before coming to the surface to breathe.

Question 82. How does a sea anemone protect itself?

Answer: A sea anemone attaches itself to rocks using its sucker-like base. This helps it remain attached despite water currents.

Question 83. What are algae?

Answer: Algae are organisms that commonly live in water or moist environments. Many algae contain chlorophyll and make food through photosynthesis.

Question 84. How do some algae attach themselves to rocks?

Answer: Some algae have root-like structures called holdfasts that help them attach to rocks and other surfaces.

8. Chapter Summary

  1. The living world has a great variety of plants, animals and other organisms. This variety is called biodiversity.
  2. Plants can be classified according to their height, stem, leaf venation, roots and seeds.
  3. Herbs are short plants with soft stems, shrubs are medium-sized woody plants and trees are tall plants with thick trunks.
  4. Creepers spread along the ground, while climbers grow upwards with support.
  5. Reticulate venation forms a network, while parallel venation has veins running parallel to each other.
  6. Taproot systems have one main root, while fibrous root systems have many thin roots.
  7. Monocot seeds have one cotyledon, while dicot seeds have two cotyledons.
  8. Animals move by walking, jumping, flying, swimming, crawling and climbing.
  9. A habitat is the natural place where an organism lives.
  10. Adaptations help organisms survive in their habitats.
  11. Camels and cactus plants have special adaptations for desert conditions.
  12. Mountain goats have thick fur and strong hooves for surviving in mountain habitats.
  13. Fish have streamlined bodies, fins and gills for living in water.
  14. Frogs are amphibians because they can live both on land and in water.
  15. Dolphins and whales breathe air through blowholes.

Message of the Chapter

The living world is full of diversity. Different plants and animals have different structures, habits and habitats. Their special adaptations help them survive in their surroundings. We should observe, understand and respect the diversity of life around us and protect all living organisms.
