6. Define the Following Terms

Question 1. What is a habitat?

Answer: A habitat is the natural home or place where a plant or animal lives.

Question 2. What is adaptation?

Answer: Adaptation is a special feature or quality that helps an organism survive in its habitat.

Question 3. What is venation?

Answer: Venation is the arrangement of veins and veinlets in a leaf.

Question 4. What are cotyledons?

Answer: Cotyledons are the seed leaves present inside a seed. They store or supply food to the developing plant.

Question 5. What are lateral roots?

Answer: Lateral roots are the smaller roots that grow from the main root.

7. Answer the Following Questions Briefly

Question 1. Why do some plants need support to grow?

Answer: Some plants have weak stems. They cannot stand upright on their own, so they need support to grow upwards. For example, pea plants and money plants.

Question 2. What is the relation between the seed of a plant, types of root and leaf venation?

Answer: Plants are generally grouped into two types on the basis of their seeds:

  1. Monocot plants: They have one cotyledon, fibrous roots and parallel venation.
  2. Dicot plants: They have two cotyledons, taproots and reticulate venation.

Question 3. Why do plants in deserts have spines in place of leaves?

Answer: Desert plants have spines instead of normal leaves to reduce water loss through transpiration. Spines also protect them from animals.

Question 4. What are the three types of habitats?

Answer: The three types of habitats are:

  1. Terrestrial habitat – Land habitat.
  2. Aquatic habitat – Water habitat.
  3. Arboreal habitat – Tree habitat.

Question 5. The trees in mountain areas have sloping branches. Why?

Answer: Mountain trees have sloping branches so that snow can slide down easily. This prevents snow from collecting on the branches and breaking them.

8. Answer the Following Questions in Detail

Question 1. Explain all the adaptations of cactus.

Answer: Cactus plants are adapted to survive in hot and dry desert conditions. Their main adaptations are:

  1. Spines instead of leaves: Their leaves are modified into spines to reduce water loss.
  2. Thick green stem: The thick stem stores water and also prepares food through photosynthesis.
  3. Waxy coating: The waxy outer layer reduces the loss of water.
  4. Long roots: Their roots spread widely or grow deep into the soil to absorb water.
  5. Reduced leaves: Modified leaves help the plant conserve water.

These adaptations help cactus plants survive in deserts where water is very limited.

Question 2. How is a frog adapted to live both on land and in water? Explain in detail.

Answer: A frog is an amphibian because it can live both on land and in water.

Its main adaptations are:

  1. Webbed feet: Its webbed hind feet help it swim in water.
  2. Strong hind legs: Its strong hind legs help it jump on land.
  3. Moist skin: Its moist skin helps it exchange gases, especially when it is in water.
  4. Lungs: Adult frogs use lungs to breathe air on land.
  5. Eyes on top of the head: Its raised eyes help it see while most of its body remains underwater.
  6. Camouflage: Its body colour helps it hide from enemies in its surroundings.

These features help a frog survive in both land and water habitats.

Question 3. List the adaptations observed in various plants and animals found in mountains.

Answer: Plants and animals living in mountains have special adaptations to survive in cold and snowy conditions.

Adaptations in mountain plants:

  1. Many trees have a cone-shaped structure.
  2. Their sloping branches allow snow to slide down easily.
  3. Many trees have needle-like leaves to reduce water loss.
  4. Thick bark protects trees from cold conditions.

Adaptations in mountain animals:

  1. Mountain goats have thick fur to keep their bodies warm.
  2. Their strong legs help them climb rocky slopes.
  3. Their rough and flexible hooves provide a good grip on rocks.
  4. Some mountain animals have thick layers of fat to protect them from cold.

Question 4. Explain the taproot and fibrous root system.

Answer: Plants have two main types of root systems: taproot and fibrous root systems.

1. Taproot System

  1. It has one thick and long main root.
  2. Smaller lateral roots arise from the main root.
  3. It generally grows deep into the soil.
  4. Examples: Mustard, carrot, sunflower and mango.

2. Fibrous Root System

  1. It has a cluster of many thin roots.
  2. These roots arise from the base of the stem.
  3. There is no single thick main root.
  4. Examples: Wheat, rice and grass.

Difference between Taproot and Fibrous Root Systems:

Taproot System Fibrous Root System
It has one main root. It has many thin roots.
Lateral roots arise from the main root. Roots are nearly equal in size.
It generally grows deeper into the soil. Roots usually spread near the soil surface.
Example: Mustard Example: Wheat

Question 5. Differentiate between reticulate and parallel venation with examples and diagrams.

Answer: Venation is the arrangement of veins in a leaf. There are two main types of venation.

1. Reticulate Venation

  1. The veins form a network-like pattern.
  2. It is commonly found in dicot plants.
  3. Examples: Mango, peepal and hibiscus.

2. Parallel Venation

  1. The veins run parallel to one another.
  2. It is commonly found in monocot plants.
  3. Examples: Grass, wheat and banana.

Difference between Reticulate and Parallel Venation:

Reticulate Venation Parallel Venation
Veins form a network. Veins run parallel to each other.
Commonly found in dicot plants. Commonly found in monocot plants.
Usually associated with taproots. Usually associated with fibrous roots.
Example: Mango Example: Wheat

9. Quick Revision – Important Points

  2. Adaptation helps an organism survive in its habitat.
  3. Herbs have soft and green stems.
  4. Shrubs are medium-sized plants with woody stems.
  5. Trees have thick and strong stems.
  6. Creepers spread along the ground.
  7. Climbers need support to grow upwards.
  8. Reticulate venation forms a network of veins.
  9. Parallel venation has parallel veins.
  10. Taproots have one main root.
  11. Fibrous roots have many thin roots.
  12. Monocot seeds have one cotyledon.
  13. Dicot seeds have two cotyledons.
  14. Cactus plants are adapted to desert conditions.
  15. Frogs can live both on land and in water.
  16. Mountain goats have strong hooves for climbing.
  17. Pine trees have sloping branches to shed snow.

10. Chapter Summary

This chapter explains the diversity of plants and animals and how they survive in different habitats.

  1. Plants can be classified as herbs, shrubs and trees.
  2. Creepers spread along the ground, while climbers grow with support.
  3. Leaves have two main types of venation: reticulate and parallel.
  4. Roots are mainly of two types: taproot and fibrous root.
  5. Monocot plants have one cotyledon, while dicot plants have two.
  6. Animals live in different habitats according to their needs.
  7. Adaptations help plants and animals survive in their surroundings.
  8. Cactus and camel are adapted to desert conditions.
  9. Frogs can live both on land and in water.
  10. Mountain plants and animals have special features to survive in cold conditions.

Message of the Chapter

The living world is full of diversity. Different plants and animals have different structures, habits and habitats. Their special adaptations help them survive in their surroundings. We should observe, understand and respect the diversity of life around us and protect all living organisms.
