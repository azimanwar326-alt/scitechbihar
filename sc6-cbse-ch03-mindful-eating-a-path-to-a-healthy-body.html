7. Define the Following Terms


Question 1. What is a food ingredient?

Answer: A food ingredient is any substance used to prepare a food item. For example, rice, lentils, salt and water are ingredients of different dishes.


Question 2. What are deficiency diseases?

Answer: Deficiency diseases are diseases caused by a lack of one or more nutrients in our diet. For example, anaemia, rickets and scurvy.


Question 3. What is a balanced diet?

Answer: A balanced diet is a diet that contains all the essential nutrients, water and dietary fibre in the right amounts.


Question 4. What is malnutrition?

Answer: Malnutrition is a condition caused by eating too little, too much or an unhealthy balance of nutrients.


Question 5. What is obesity?

Answer: Obesity is a condition in which a person has excessive body fat. It can increase the risk of diseases such as diabetes and heart problems.

8. Answer the Following Questions Briefly


Question 1. Why do we need food?

Answer: We need food for the following reasons:

  1. It gives us energy to do daily activities.
  2. It helps our body grow.
  3. It repairs damaged body parts.
  4. It protects us from diseases.
  5. It keeps our body healthy.

Question 2. Why is the breakdown of carbohydrates important before they are absorbed?

Answer: Carbohydrates are large food molecules. They must be broken down into simple sugars, such as glucose, before they can be absorbed into the blood. The body uses glucose to get energy.


Question 3. How are saturated fats different from unsaturated fats?

Answer: Saturated fats are usually solid at room temperature and are commonly found in butter, ghee and fatty meat. Unsaturated fats are usually liquid at room temperature and are found in nuts, seeds and vegetable oils.

Saturated Fats Unsaturated Fats
Usually solid at room temperature. Usually liquid at room temperature.
Found in butter, ghee and fatty meat. Found in nuts, seeds and vegetable oils.
Too much intake may increase the risk of heart disease. Generally considered healthier when used instead of saturated fats.

Question 4. What is the importance of proteins in our diet?

Answer: Proteins are body-building nutrients. They help our body grow and repair damaged tissues. They also help in making muscles, enzymes and other important body substances.


Question 5. Why is it important to take a balanced diet?

Answer: A balanced diet is important because it provides all the nutrients needed by our body. It gives us energy, helps us grow, repairs body tissues and protects us from deficiency diseases. It also helps us remain healthy and active.

9. Answer the Following Questions in Detail

Question 1. With the help of a lab activity, show the presence of starch in a slice of bread.

Answer: We can test for the presence of starch in bread by using iodine solution.

Materials Required:

  • A slice of bread
  • Iodine solution
  • A dropper
  • A small plate

Procedure:

  1. Take a small piece of bread and place it on a plate.
  2. Put two or three drops of iodine solution on the bread.
  3. Observe the colour of the bread.

Observation: The part of the bread where iodine solution is added turns blue-black.

Conclusion: The blue-black colour shows that starch is present in the bread.


Question 2. What is the importance of vitamins in our diet? How are fat-soluble vitamins different from water-soluble vitamins?

Answer: Vitamins are protective nutrients that help our body remain healthy. They are needed in small amounts for the proper functioning of the body.

Importance of Vitamins:

  1. Vitamin A helps us maintain healthy eyes and skin.
  2. Vitamin B helps the body release energy from food and supports the nervous system.
  3. Vitamin C helps in wound healing and keeps gums healthy.
  4. Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and keeps bones strong.
  5. Vitamin K helps in blood clotting.

Difference between Fat-Soluble and Water-Soluble Vitamins:

Fat-Soluble Vitamins Water-Soluble Vitamins
They dissolve in fats. They dissolve in water.
They can be stored in the body, mainly in the liver and fatty tissues. Most are not stored in large amounts in the body.
They include vitamins A, D, E and K. They include the vitamin B group and vitamin C.
They do not need to be consumed every day in the same way as water-soluble vitamins. They generally need regular intake through food.

Question 3. Discuss the importance of dietary fibre and water in our diet.

Answer: Dietary fibre and water are essential for maintaining good health.

Importance of Dietary Fibre:

  1. It helps food move through the digestive system.
  2. It prevents constipation.
  3. It keeps our digestive system healthy.
  4. It helps us feel full for a longer time.
  5. Fruits, vegetables, whole grains and pulses are good sources of fibre.

Importance of Water:

  1. Water helps in digestion.
  2. It helps transport nutrients throughout the body.
  3. It regulates body temperature.
  4. It helps remove waste products through urine and sweat.
  5. It prevents dehydration.

Therefore, we should eat fibre-rich foods and drink enough clean water every day.


Question 4. Write a note on deficiency diseases caused by nutrients.

Answer: Deficiency diseases are caused when our body does not get enough of a particular nutrient for a long time.

Some common deficiency diseases are:

Disease Nutrient Deficiency Main Symptoms
Night blindness Vitamin A Difficulty seeing in dim light.
Beriberi Vitamin B1 Weakness and nerve problems.
Scurvy Vitamin C Swollen and bleeding gums.
Rickets Vitamin D Weak and soft bones.
Anaemia Iron Tiredness, weakness and pale skin.
Goitre Iodine Swelling in the neck.

We can prevent many deficiency diseases by eating a balanced diet containing fruits, vegetables, cereals, pulses, milk and other nutritious foods.


Question 5. Discuss the functions of the following minerals.

Answer: Minerals are important nutrients required by our body in small or large amounts. They help in the proper growth and functioning of the body.

1. Calcium

  • It helps build strong bones and teeth.
  • It helps muscles work properly.
  • It plays an important role in blood clotting.

2. Iron

  • It helps in the formation of haemoglobin in the blood.
  • Haemoglobin carries oxygen to different parts of the body.
  • Iron deficiency can cause anaemia.

3. Sodium and Potassium

  • They help maintain the balance of water in the body.
  • They help nerves send messages.
  • They help muscles work properly.

4. Iodine

  • It helps the thyroid gland make thyroid hormones.
  • These hormones control growth and metabolism.
  • Iodine deficiency can cause goitre.

5. Phosphorus

  • It helps build strong bones and teeth.
  • It helps the body produce and use energy.
  • It is needed for the proper functioning of cells.

6. Fluorine

  • Fluoride helps strengthen tooth enamel.
  • It protects teeth from tooth decay.
  • It is commonly present in drinking water and some foods.

Question 6. How have the culinary practices of India changed over time?

Answer: The culinary practices of India have changed over time because of changes in lifestyle, technology, travel and food habits.

  1. Traditional Cooking: Earlier, people mostly cooked food at home using fresh and locally available ingredients.
  2. Regional Food: Different regions developed their own special dishes using local grains, vegetables, spices and cooking methods.
  3. Modern Kitchen Appliances: Gas stoves, pressure cookers, refrigerators and mixers have made cooking easier and faster.
  4. Packaged and Processed Foods: Ready-to-eat foods, instant noodles, packaged snacks and frozen foods have become more common.
  5. Fast Food Culture: Pizza, burgers and other fast foods have become popular, especially in cities.
  6. Health Awareness: Many people are now becoming more aware of balanced diets, food labels, fresh foods and healthy cooking methods.

Thus, Indian food habits have changed from mainly traditional home cooking to a mixture of traditional and modern food practices. However, healthy traditional foods such as dal, rice, roti, vegetables, curd and seasonal fruits remain important.
